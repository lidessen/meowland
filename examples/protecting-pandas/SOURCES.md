## Original six-page teaching adaptation plan

The following is an adjustable classroom organization suggestion of about 20 minutes, depending on students' familiarity with the original reading; it does not claim to cover
all goals of the whole Reading lesson. Each person first forms their own expression, then checks with a partner; when a few students report, other students also mark evidence or revise one sentence.

| Stable page id | What students do and leave behind                                                                                                      | Evidence and feedback                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `welcome`      | Read the goals, raise one question they want to answer; distinguish predictions from facts already read                                | The original image is only a lead-in; do not judge wild panda status from the photo, and do not impersonate an official invitation or protection qualification award. |
| `growth`       | Choose a growth stage, retell the change in one sentence, and point out the age or time expression                                     | G1/G2/G3 plus facts supplemented from the text; it is not a sequencing question and is not graded against a single wording.                                           |
| `problems`     | Choose a problem, write or say its consequences, and mark the supporting sentence                                                      | P1/P2/P3; student expression comes first, then teacher key words or reference relationships.                                                                          |
| `protect`      | Choose a measure, explain which problem it responds to and why                                                                         | A1/A2 against the P-type excerpts; evidence-based different associations are accepted, without forcing three exclusive pairs.                                         |
| `meaning`      | Explain why protection is still needed despite existing help, and try to explain what hope means                                       | M1 combined with growth and action; `meaningReference` is a reference explanation, not the original text or the only answer.                                          |
| `act`          | Each person writes one concrete action they can actually complete, stating time and evidence; a partner checks feasibility and reasons | Open drafts with retractable sentence frames; only explicit redo clears them, with no automatic scoring or title issuing.                                             |

Hints, reference expressions and sentence frames are shown separately as needed; after retracting scaffolds, the checkable original text and drafts remain, and asking for help again does not clear answers.
Frames such as "I will ___ this week because the reading says ___." are teacher adaptation, not textbook sentences.
When students propose making a poster, introducing the reading or saving resources, explain the relationship to the learning content; do not claim that one poster or saving one sheet of paper
has directly improved wild panda numbers or protected a specific bamboo forest.

The institution attribution on original PPT P2 and "Honorary Panda Keepers" on P3/P16 belong to its classroom context; official authorization was not verified in this round,
and the new work does not treat them as a real invitation or qualification. P13's "who was saved by people" is an explanation-layer interpretation; this work, based on the original text, only says
Xiwang grew with care and does not add a wild rescue experience.

This continues original PPT P7–9's time words/growth retelling, P10–11's text problems-consequences-measures, and the research organization of each person observing first, peers expressing, and listeners checking. Two limited segments were added; the original six pages, five-question choices and Hint/Reference/Evidence progressive scaffolds are kept.

| Wolong cub and Gao Gao two photos (`observe`) | Each person describes one visible detail; compare color, eyes and posture; move to `growth` and retell Xiwang's changes with G1–G3 ages and time words                                  | The two photos are different individuals with different framing ratios, so size cannot imply age or weight; they are not four-stage life photos. Suggest about 1–2 minutes for each of the two segments, observing independently first and then saying one sentence to a partner, reducing playback/sharing according to the class; do not equate longer media with fuller learning.
